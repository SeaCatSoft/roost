Benchmarks

Your numbers, held against the standard.

A value that looks ordinary alone gets flagged the moment it drifts from what the breed and the industry expect.

  • Feed conversionModern broiler objectives sit near 1.55–1.65 at 42 days. This planner implies 1.90 — the largest single lever on the page.
  • MortalityA healthy 42-day cycle generally runs 3–5%. The plan assumes 5%; sustained days above trend raise a flag.
  • Seven-day weightChicks are widely expected near 4–4.5× day-old weight by day 7 — the earliest honest signal of how the cycle ends.
  • Brooding temperatureAround 32–34 °C at placement, stepping down some 2–3 °C a week toward 21 °C, read from the birds as much as the thermometer.
  • Water to feedTypically 1.7–2.0 : 1 by weight. Drinking falls before birds go off feed.
  • Litter moistureHeld near 20–25%. Damp litter is the standard precursor to footpad and hock lesions — which is why the daily check asks.
  • Dressing yieldWhole-bird yields commonly fall between 70–75%. Your 72% sits mid-band.
  • Vaccination windowsNewcastle and IB near day 5–7, Gumboro near day 14 are common patterns. Roost holds the schedule and defers to your vet — as your workbook already notes.

Reference ranges come from published breed performance objectives and standard extension guidance. They are starting defaults — every figure is editable per farm, and health decisions belong with your veterinarian.

Questions

Straight answers.

Do I have to give up my spreadsheet?

No. Roost imports the planner you already use — placements, mortality, intake curves, cost assumptions, sales mix, prices — and every one stays editable. Most farms run both for a cycle before trusting the app alone, and a full cycle exports back out whenever you want it.

How much work is the daily check?

Eight fields, most a single tap. Bird counts carry forward on their own; the only things you type are mortality and, on weighing days, a handful of sample weights. It is built to be filled in standing in the house, one-handed.

What exactly do the forecasts forecast?

Three things: live weight at your target age, feed still to buy, and end-of-cycle margin at today's prices. They draw on your own history once a cycle or two is on file, and on published breed curves before that. They are projections, not promises, and Roost shows the assumptions behind each one.

Will the assistant email customers on its own?

Not unless you tell it to. It drafts a reminder in the tone you set and leaves it in a queue until you approve it. Review, edit, delete, or switch it off per customer. The database itself refuses to record a send without a recorded approval.

Where does the data live?

In PostgreSQL, with the site served as a static build. Every table — cycles, daily checks, feed purchases, processing runs, assets, invoices — exports to CSV or a spreadsheet on demand. There is no lock-in.
